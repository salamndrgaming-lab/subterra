# Filing a federal mining claim — the real-world process

This is what you do *after* Subterra surfaces an open BLM cell and you
decide to stake it. Subterra generates the paperwork; you do the field
work and the mailing. None of this is legal advice.

## 1. Confirm the land is open (Subterra has already checked)

- Surface managed by BLM
- No active claim already filed (run "Check claim conflicts" in the app)
- Not inside a designated withdrawal (Wilderness, National Monument, etc.)
- Not on Native American trust land

The "Open BLM Land" layer in the sidebar pre-computes this. The
opportunity score gives you a 0–100 ranking with rationale.

## 2. Discovery

Federal mining law requires a **valuable mineral deposit discovery** on
the claim. In practice for the Notice-of-Location filing, BLM accepts
your written assertion. For long-term defensibility, document it
(geologic samples, assay reports, photographs).

## 3. Set monuments

A federal lode claim is 1500′ × 600′ (≈ 20 acres). A placer is up to
160 acres in 20-acre subdivisions.

You must physically mark the corners. Acceptable monuments:
- 4×4 wooden post, 4′ tall, with claim name + locator name + date in waterproof marker
- Pile of rocks ≥ 18″ tall with a metal tag, OR
- Steel rebar with metal tag (in rocky ground)

Subterra's NoL packet includes a corner-checklist PDF you tick off as
you set each one. Use the app's camera tool to geotag a photo at each
corner.

## 4. Post Notice of Location at the discovery monument

The discovery monument is where the valuable deposit was found
(usually a corner or the geographic center). Post the same Notice of
Location form there.

## 5. Record with the county

Within **90 days of discovery**, file a certified copy of the Notice
of Location with the County Recorder of the county where the claim
sits. Recording fees vary by county (typically $10–$50).

Subterra's NoL packet includes a pre-filled county recordation cover
sheet with the county address, recorder fee, and parcel APN if known.

## 6. File with BLM

Within the same **90 days**, file with the BLM state office that has
jurisdiction (NV, AZ, CA, CO, etc.). Required:

- Form 3830-2 (Notice of Location of Mining Claim) — pre-filled
- $20 recordation processing fee per claim
- $200 location fee per claim
- Self-addressed envelope

Subterra's NoL packet includes the right BLM state office mailing
address and a fee-voucher template. You add your check or money order.

## 7. Annual maintenance

After September 1 of the year *after* you file, owe BLM:

- $165 per claim per year, OR
- File a "small miner" waiver if you and affiliates hold ≤ 10 claims (waiver reduces fee but adds annual labor / improvements requirement)

Subterra's alert system (Phase 6) reminds you 30 days before each
deadline.

## 8. What can go wrong

- Missed the 90-day filing deadline → claim is void.
- Monument not visible or improperly marked → claim is void against a
  subsequent locator who marks better.
- Annual maintenance not paid → claim is forfeited.

The whole point of Subterra is to keep you on top of these dates and
to surface opportunities others have missed.

## Money model

A claim costs you ~$220 to file (location fee + recordation) plus
$165/yr to maintain.

Three exit paths:

1. **Hold and sell** to a junior explorer — typical price $2k–$50k per
   claim depending on commodity / proximity to producers.
2. **Mine yourself** — under federal law you have surface use rights
   for mining purposes. Small-scale recreational mining is permitted.
3. **Lease to an operator** — they pay rent + royalty. Royalty rates
   for hard-rock vary, often 2–5% of net smelter return.
